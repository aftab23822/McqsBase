## How to revise this NAT-ICS Boolean Algebra and Digital Logic set

1. When converting binary to decimal, write each bit's power-of-two weight before adding; this helps catch place-value errors.
2. For a Boolean expression, simplify one law at a time and check the result against a truth-table row if uncertain.
3. Memorise the distinction between XOR and OR: XOR is 1 only when the inputs differ, while OR is 1 when at least one input is 1.
4. For NAND and NOR questions, first evaluate the underlying AND or OR, then invert its result.
5. For circuit questions, label each intermediate gate output before calculating the final output.
6. Review the assumptions in sequential-logic questions: a flip-flop stores state, so its next output can depend on its previous state.
