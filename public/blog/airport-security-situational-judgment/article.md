Airport Security Situational Judgment MCQs Practice Set for November 13, 2026

Airport-security work calls for careful observation, consistent rule-following, calm communication, and prompt escalation when something seems unsafe. This independent practice set develops those judgment skills through scenarios involving unattended belongings, restricted access, screening interactions, incident reporting, teamwork, and integrity. The situations are learning exercises, not official ASF procedures; follow the latest official notice and applicable instructions for actual recruitment or duty requirements.

## How to revise this Airport Security Situational Judgment set

1. For each scenario, identify the immediate safety concern before choosing an action; avoid touching, moving, or investigating an item when the safer choice is to report it and follow instructions.
2. When access or screening rules are involved, prefer consistent authorization checks over assumptions, familiarity, urgency, or a person's claimed status.
3. Practise separating what you directly observed from what you inferred. Reports should state concrete details such as location, time, appearance, and actions.
4. Review which options preserve safety and accountability: notifying the appropriate supervisor, following established procedure, and recording relevant facts are often stronger than acting alone.
5. Notice tempting but weak choices in the set, such as arguing, sharing restricted information, ignoring a small discrepancy, or making an exception to avoid inconvenience.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are these questions official ASF test questions?

No. They are original, independent practice scenarios and are not presented as official questions, past-paper items, or a confirmed outline of the ASF recruitment test.

### What is the best way to review a situational judgment question?

Compare each option against safety, consistent rule-following, respectful communication, and appropriate escalation. Then explain why the selected action is better than the most tempting alternative.

### Should I treat these scenarios as airport operating instructions?

No. They teach general judgment principles only. Actual duties and procedures depend on current official instructions, training, and the circumstances of the situation.

### You notice a small bag left unattended beside a busy waiting-area seat. What is the best first response?

Keep a safe distance and promptly notify the appropriate security supervisor or response channel, giving the exact location.

### A colleague suggests moving an unattended parcel to clear a walkway. What should you do?

Do not move it; report its location and follow the instructions of the responsible security personnel.

### You see a person repeatedly looking around and leaving a bag behind, but you do not know their intent. What is the soundest action?

Report the observable behavior and location without claiming to know the person's motive.

### A bag is unattended, but a nearby passenger says it belongs to their companion. What should you do?

Do not rely only on an unverified explanation; report the situation and follow the applicable instructions.
