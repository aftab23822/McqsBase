## How to revise this NTS Analytical Reasoning Practice set

1. For a sequence, write the differences between consecutive terms before guessing a rule. Check that the rule explains every step.
2. In deduction questions, use only the stated premises. A conclusion that could be true is not necessarily one that must be true.
3. Rewrite conditional statements as short arrows, such as rain → match cancelled, and check whether the question gives the condition or its consequence.
4. For ordering puzzles, sketch slots and place fixed or adjacent items first; then test every remaining clue against the completed order.
5. Show units and intermediate steps in rate, percentage, and average problems. This helps catch errors caused by mixing minutes with hours or totals with averages.
6. After selecting an option, substitute it back into all the clues. This is especially useful for multi-step arrangement questions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are these questions official NTS questions?

No. They are original independent practice questions and are not presented as official questions, past-paper items, or a confirmed NTS syllabus.

### What should I do if an actual test has a different reasoning format?

Use these questions to practise transferable reasoning skills, then consult the latest official test notice for the format and requirements that apply to your test.

### How should I review a question I answered incorrectly?

First identify the clue or rule you missed, then solve it again without looking at the explanation. For a puzzle, write the constraints down; for a sequence, compare consecutive differences; for a calculation, check the units and arithmetic.

### Should I guess when I cannot identify a sequence rule?

Try simple checks first: differences, ratios, alternating operations, and familiar square or factorial patterns. If no rule explains all given terms, avoid choosing a rule that fits only the last step.
