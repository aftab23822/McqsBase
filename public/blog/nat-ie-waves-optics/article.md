## How to revise this NAT-IE Waves and Optics set

1. For wave calculations, write down the relationship v = fλ and convert units before substituting values.
2. Keep frequency and period distinct: frequency is cycles per second, while period is seconds per cycle; they are reciprocals.
3. For refraction questions, identify which medium has the greater refractive index before predicting the ray's direction.
4. For mirror and lens problems, state the sign convention you are using and check whether the image should be real or virtual.
5. Review missed questions by concept, especially when the error came from mixing up wavelength, amplitude, frequency, or image orientation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are these questions official NAT-IE questions?

No. They are original, independent practice questions written in an entry-test style and are not copied from an official paper.

### Which formulas are most useful for this practice set?

Useful relationships include v = fλ, T = 1/f, n = c/v, Snell's law n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂, and the thin-lens or mirror equation 1/f = 1/do + 1/di. Check the sign convention specified in each question.

### How should I approach a question about a ray entering another medium?

Compare the refractive indices. The ray bends toward the normal when it enters a higher-index medium and away from the normal when it enters a lower-index medium; at normal incidence, its direction does not change.

### What should I do if my answer differs from the explanation?

Recheck units, the relationship being used, and whether the question describes frequency, period, image distance, or object distance. For ray diagrams, mark the normal or principal axis before deciding the result.
