## How to revise this NAT-IE Chemical Equilibrium and Kinetics set

1. For each equilibrium question, first balance the equation and write the expression before substituting values; omit pure solids and liquids from K.
2. When comparing Q and K, state the direction explicitly: Q < K means net forward reaction, while Q > K means net reverse reaction.
3. Separate the effect of changing temperature from other disturbances: only temperature changes the value of K for a given reaction.
4. For rate-law questions, compare experiments in which only one reactant concentration changes; use the rate ratio to find its order.
5. Check units for time and concentration before calculating k, and remember that the units of k depend on the overall reaction order.
6. For mechanisms, derive the rate law from the slow elementary step only when its molecularity and any preceding fast-step assumptions support that expression.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are these official NAT-IE questions or confirmed syllabus questions?

No. They are original, independent practice questions in an NAT-IE-style chemistry scope. Test formats and content can vary, so consult the latest official notice for current arrangements.

### What is a useful way to work through the equilibrium calculations?

Write the balanced equation and its K expression, record initial and changing amounts in a table, then substitute equilibrium values. Check that concentrations remain physically possible and that the result is consistent with Q and K.

### How should I distinguish a change in K from a shift in equilibrium?

A disturbance can shift the composition while leaving K unchanged. For a fixed reaction, changing temperature changes K; concentration, pressure, volume, or a catalyst does not change K.

### Why do rate-constant units vary between questions?

The rate law includes concentration raised to the overall order. Since rate is concentration per time, the units of k must compensate for the concentration powers in that rate law.
