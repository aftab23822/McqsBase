It progresses from moles and formula masses to balanced-equation calculations, limiting reagents, solution concentrations, percentage yield, and gas volumes. For calculations, use the rounded atomic masses H=1, C=12, N=14, O=16, Na=23, Mg=24, Al=27, S=32, Cl=35.5, K=39, Ca=40, Fe=56, Zn=65, and Cu=63.5 unless a question gives another value. For gas-volume questions, STP means 0°C and 1 atm, where one mole of an ideal gas occupies 22.4 L. ## How to revise this NAT-IE Stoichiometry set

1. Balance the equation before using mole ratios; coefficients, not subscripts, give the reacting mole proportions.
2. Write the conversion path explicitly: grams to moles, mole ratio, then moles to the requested unit.
3. For limiting-reagent questions, compare available moles divided by each reactant's coefficient; the smaller usable amount identifies the limiting reactant.
4. Keep solution volumes in litres when calculating molarity, and use M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ only when the amount of solute stays constant during dilution.
5. Check that percentage yield is actual yield divided by theoretical yield, multiplied by 100, and that the result cannot exceed 100% unless the data or product purity needs investigation.
6. Use 22.4 L mol⁻¹ only for the explicitly stated STP convention in this set; do not assume the same gas volume at other conditions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the most important first step in a stoichiometry calculation?

Write and balance the chemical equation. Its coefficients provide the mole ratios needed to connect reactants and products.

### How do I decide which reactant is limiting?

Convert the reactants to moles, then compare each amount with its coefficient in the balanced equation. The reactant that runs out first limits the amount of product.

### When can I use M₁V₁ = M₂V₂?

Use it for a dilution in which solvent is added but the amount of dissolved solute remains unchanged. Express both volumes in the same units.

### Why does this set specify STP for gas-volume questions?

Gas molar volume depends on temperature and pressure. The stated convention makes the 22.4 L per mole conversion unambiguous for these questions.

No. It is independent stoichiometry practice and does not establish official test coverage. Check the latest official notice for current test information.
