## How to revise this NAT-IE Thermal Physics and Thermodynamics set

1. Convert Celsius temperatures to kelvin before using gas laws or Carnot efficiency; temperature differences in Celsius and kelvin have the same numerical size.
2. Write the known quantities and the relevant equation before substituting values, especially in calorimetry and gas-law problems.
3. For phase changes, use Q = mL; temperature stays constant during the idealized change of state even though heat is transferred.

5. Review why conduction, convection, and radiation differ, and practise identifying the dominant transfer mechanism in each stated situation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do gas-law questions use kelvin rather than Celsius?

Gas-law relationships use absolute temperature, whose zero corresponds to the lower limit of thermal energy. Convert with T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15 before substituting.

### When should I use specific heat capacity and when should I use latent heat?

Use Q = mcΔT when a substance changes temperature without changing state. Use Q = mL for an idealized change of state at constant temperature.

### What does positive work mean in the thermodynamics questions here?

The convention is work done by the gas. Expansion against an external pressure is positive work; compression means work done by the gas is negative.
