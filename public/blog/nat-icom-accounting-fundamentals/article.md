## How to revise this NAT-ICOM Accounting Fundamentals Practice set

1. For every transaction, write the two affected accounts first, then decide whether each account increases or decreases before assigning debits and credits.
2. When solving an accounting-equation question, check that assets equal liabilities plus equity after the transaction; distinguish a cash-for-equipment exchange from a new asset purchase on credit.
3. Keep periodic inventory formulas visible: cost of goods sold equals opening inventory plus net purchases minus closing inventory.
4. For straight-line depreciation, subtract residual value before dividing by useful life; accumulated depreciation is not the same as the asset’s original cost.
5. Label the numerator and denominator before calculating a ratio, and convert a percentage to a decimal only at the final step.
6. On trial-balance questions, separate arithmetic agreement from completeness: some omissions and wrong-account postings do not make the debit and credit totals unequal.
7. For break-even questions, calculate contribution per unit or contribution-margin ratio first, then divide fixed costs by that contribution measure.

### Do I need a calculator for this set?

The arithmetic is designed to be manageable by hand, though a calculator can help you check division and percentages. Show the formula and intermediate steps so you can identify whether an error came from the accounting rule or the arithmetic.

### Why can a trial balance agree even when the books contain an error?

A trial balance checks whether total debits equal total credits. It does not detect every error: for example, a transaction omitted entirely or posted to the wrong account on the correct side may leave the totals equal.
