## How to revise this NAT-IE Engineering Physics: Mechanics set

1. Write down the known quantities and convert them to SI units before choosing a formula; kilometres per hour and centimetres are frequent sources of errors.
2. For motion questions, define a positive direction first. Displacement and velocity can be negative even when distance and speed are non-negative.
3. Draw a free-body diagram before applying Newton's second law. Include only forces acting on the object being analysed.
4. Check whether a friction question describes static or kinetic friction; maximum static friction is a limit, not always the actual friction force.
5. Use the work-energy theorem when forces change speed, and remember that only the component of force along displacement contributes to work.
6. For collisions, distinguish momentum conservation from kinetic-energy conservation; kinetic energy is not generally conserved in an inelastic collision.
7. In circular motion, centripetal force is the net inward force, not an extra kind of force to add to a force diagram.
