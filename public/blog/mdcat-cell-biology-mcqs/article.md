| 1 | Which description best fits the fluid mosaic model of a cell membrane?<br />A) A rigid layer of cellulose with embedded DNA<br />B) A phospholipid bilayer containing mobile proteins<br />C) A single protein sheet surrounding the cytoplasm<br />D) Two carbohydrate layers joined by ribosomes | **B) A phospholipid bilayer containing mobile proteins**<br />The membrane consists mainly of a phospholipid bilayer with proteins embedded in or associated with it. | 2 | In a phospholipid bilayer, where do the hydrophilic heads normally face?<br />A) Toward the watery environments inside and outside the cell<br />B) Toward the center of the bilayer only<br />C) Into the hydrophobic tails of neighboring lipids<br />D) Away from both the cytoplasm and extracellular fluid | **A) Toward the watery environments inside and outside the cell**<br />Phospholipid heads interact with water, so they face the cytoplasm and extracellular fluid. | 3 | Which substance crosses a pure phospholipid bilayer most readily by simple diffusion?<br />A) A large starch molecule<br />B) A sodium ion<br />C) A small nonpolar oxygen molecule<br />D) A charged amino acid | **C) A small nonpolar oxygen molecule**<br />Small nonpolar molecules can pass through the hydrophobic interior of the bilayer relatively easily. | 4 | What is the principal role of cholesterol in many animal cell membranes?<br />A) It forms the cell's hereditary material<br />B) It helps moderate membrane fluidity and stability<br />C) It pumps glucose directly across the membrane<br />D) It replaces all membrane phospholipids | **B) It helps moderate membrane fluidity and stability**<br />Cholesterol fits between phospholipids and helps regulate membrane fluidity and stability. | 5 | A membrane protein that spans the entire phospholipid bilayer is called what?<br />A) Integral protein<br />B) Peripheral protein<br />C) Histone<br />D) Cellulose protein | **A) Integral protein**<br />Integral proteins are embedded in the membrane, and some extend across its full width. | 6 | Which membrane component commonly helps cells recognize one another?<br />A) Carbohydrate chains attached to proteins or lipids<br />B) Free DNA strands in the bilayer<br />C) Cellulose fibers embedded in every animal membrane<br />D) Ribosomal RNA on the outer surface | **A) Carbohydrate chains attached to proteins or lipids**<br />Membrane glycoproteins and glycolipids can act as cell-recognition markers. | 7 | A membrane allows water through but blocks a particular solute. What property does this demonstrate?<br />A) Selective permeability<br />B) Contractility<br />C) Chromosome condensation<br />D) Enzyme denaturation | **A) Selective permeability**<br />Selective permeability means that a membrane permits some substances to cross more readily than others. It enables cells to regulate their internal conditions. |
| 8 | A molecule moves from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration without a transport protein. What process is occurring?<br />A) Simple diffusion<br />B) Active transport<br />C) Endocytosis<br />D) Exocytosis | **A) Simple diffusion**<br />Simple diffusion is net movement down a concentration gradient without a membrane transport protein or direct ATP input. |
| 9 | Glucose moves down its concentration gradient through a carrier protein. Which process is this?<br />A) Facilitated diffusion<br />B) Primary active transport<br />C) Phagocytosis<br />D) Osmosis | **A) Facilitated diffusion**<br />Facilitated diffusion uses a membrane protein to help a substance move down its gradient. It does not directly require ATP, unlike active transport. |
| 10 | A cell uses ATP to move calcium ions against their concentration gradient. This is an example of what?<br />A) Simple diffusion<br />B) Osmosis<br />C) Active transport<br />D) Facilitated diffusion | **C) Active transport**<br />Movement against a concentration gradient requires energy. A membrane pump can use ATP to move calcium ions in this direction. |
| 11 | Which description defines osmosis?<br />A) Net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane<br />B) Movement of solute particles through a membrane protein<br />C) Engulfment of a particle by the cell membrane<br />D) Release of vesicle contents outside a cell | **A) Net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane**<br />Osmosis is the net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane. It occurs in response to differences in water availability across the membrane. |
| 12 | A red blood cell is placed in a solution with a lower solute concentration than its cytoplasm. What is the likely result?<br />A) Water enters the cell, which may swell<br />B) Water leaves the cell, which shrinks<br />C) No net water movement occurs<br />D) The cell immediately forms a cell wall | **A) Water enters the cell, which may swell**<br />The surrounding solution is hypotonic relative to the cell, so water moves into the cell by osmosis. An animal cell may swell or burst because it lacks a cell wall. |
| 13 | An animal cell is placed in a solution with a higher solute concentration than its cytoplasm. What is the expected effect?<br />A) Water leaves the cell and it shrinks<br />B) Water enters the cell and it swells<br />C) The cell develops a large central vacuole<br />D) The solute concentration inside immediately becomes zero | **A) Water leaves the cell and it shrinks**<br />The surrounding solution is hypertonic, so water moves out of the cell by osmosis. The loss of water causes an animal cell to shrink. |
| 14 | A plant cell placed in distilled water becomes firm rather than bursting. Which structure most directly limits excessive expansion?<br />A) Cell wall<br />B) Nucleolus<br />C) Ribosome<br />D) Golgi apparatus | **A) Cell wall**<br />Water entering the plant cell increases turgor pressure, but the rigid cell wall resists expansion. This support helps prevent the cell from bursting. |
| 15 | What is the main function of the sodium-potassium pump in an animal cell?<br />A) To move sodium and potassium ions across the membrane using energy<br />B) To copy DNA before cell division<br />C) To digest proteins in the cytoplasm<br />D) To move water through a channel without any ion movement | **A) To move sodium and potassium ions across the membrane using energy**<br />The sodium-potassium pump is an active transporter that uses ATP to move ions against their gradients. In a typical cycle it exports three sodium ions and imports two potassium ions. |
| 16 | A cell takes in a large particle by surrounding it with its plasma membrane. What is this process called?<br />A) Endocytosis<br />B) Exocytosis<br />C) Diffusion<br />D) Transcription | **A) Endocytosis**<br />During endocytosis, the membrane encloses material and forms a vesicle that enters the cell. Exocytosis releases material from a cell. |
| 17 | A secretory vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane and releases its contents outside the cell. Which process is involved?<br />A) Exocytosis<br />B) Osmosis<br />C) Phagocytosis<br />D) DNA replication | **A) Exocytosis**<br />In exocytosis, a vesicle merges with the plasma membrane and discharges its contents outside the cell. This is commonly used to release secreted products. |
| 18 | A white blood cell surrounds and internalizes a bacterium. Which form of endocytosis is this?<br />A) Phagocytosis<br />B) Pinocytosis<br />C) Facilitated diffusion<br />D) Exocytosis | **A) Phagocytosis**<br />Phagocytosis is the engulfment of relatively large particles, such as bacteria, by a cell. Pinocytosis instead takes in extracellular fluid and dissolved substances. |
| 19 | What is a typical function of an aquaporin?<br />A) To provide a channel through which water can cross a membrane<br />B) To synthesize DNA in the nucleus<br />C) To break down hydrogen peroxide<br />D) To attach amino acids to transfer RNA | **A) To provide a channel through which water can cross a membrane**<br />Aquaporins are membrane channel proteins that facilitate water movement. They do not synthesize DNA or perform the functions of peroxisomes or ribosomes. |
| 20 | A substance crosses a membrane through a channel protein from high concentration to low concentration. Which statement is correct?<br />A) The movement is facilitated diffusion and is down the gradient<br />B) The movement is active transport because a protein is involved<br />C) The movement is osmosis regardless of the substance<br />D) The movement must use ATP because it crosses a membrane | **A) The movement is facilitated diffusion and is down the gradient**<br />A channel can enable facilitated diffusion, but protein use alone does not make transport active. Movement down the gradient does not directly require ATP. |

## How to revise this MDCAT Cell Biology set

1. When a transport question describes movement down a concentration gradient, check whether a membrane protein is needed; facilitated diffusion uses a protein but does not directly require ATP.
2. For osmosis, identify the solute concentration on each side and state the direction of water movement before predicting cell size.
3. Review organelles by linking each one to a job, such as protein secretion, lipid synthesis, intracellular digestion or detoxification.
4. In cell-cycle questions, distinguish chromosome number from DNA quantity: DNA is replicated in S phase, while sister chromatids remain joined until they separate.
5. For microscope calculations, use magnification = image size ÷ actual size and keep units consistent; magnification does not itself improve resolution.

First answer each item without looking at the key. For every mistake, write down the underlying rule—such as the direction of osmosis or the event in a cell-cycle phase—and then retry similar questions.

### What is the main difference between facilitated diffusion and active transport?

Facilitated diffusion moves substances down their concentration or electrochemical gradient through membrane proteins without directly using ATP. Active transport moves a substance against its gradient and requires an energy source.

### Why do cell-division questions distinguish chromosomes from chromatids?

After DNA replication, each chromosome consists of two sister chromatids joined at a centromere. The chromatids become separate chromosomes when they move apart during division.
