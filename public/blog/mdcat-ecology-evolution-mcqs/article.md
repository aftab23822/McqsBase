| 1 | A group of individuals of the same species living in the same area at the same time is a:<br />A) Community<br />B) Population<br />C) Ecosystem<br />D) Biome | **B) Population**<br />A population consists of members of one species in a defined area and period. | 8 | A population in which individuals occur in groups around patches of food has which dispersion pattern?<br />A) Clumped<br />B) Uniform<br />C) Random<br />D) Linear growth | **A) Clumped**<br />Clumped dispersion occurs when resources or suitable conditions are concentrated in patches, drawing individuals together. |
| 9 | A population increases by 30 individuals over one year, with no immigration or emigration. What is the net effect of births minus deaths?<br />A) A decrease of 30<br />B) An increase of 30<br />C) No change<br />D) It cannot be inferred | **B) An increase of 30**<br />With migration absent, the change in population size is births minus deaths. | 10 | Which expression gives population change when migration is included?<br />A) Births + deaths + immigration − emigration<br />B) Births − deaths + immigration − emigration<br />C) Deaths − births + emigration − immigration<br />D) Births − immigration + deaths + emigration | **B) Births − deaths + immigration − emigration**<br />Births and immigration add individuals; deaths and emigration remove them. | 11 | Under ideal conditions with resources continually available, a population's per-capita growth rate is constant. | 12 | A logistic growth curve typically levels off near the:<br />A) Mutation rate<br />B) Carrying capacity<br />C) Trophic efficiency<br />D) Niche breadth | **B) Carrying capacity**<br />Carrying capacity is the largest population an environment can sustain over time under prevailing conditions. |
| 13 | A population's carrying capacity falls after a prolonged drought. | 14 | Which is a density-dependent factor that can reduce population growth?<br />A) A volcanic eruption<br />B) A severe frost affecting the whole region<br />C) Competition for food among crowded individuals<br />D) A sudden flood | **C) Competition for food among crowded individuals**<br />Competition often intensifies as population density rises. | 15 | A storm kills a similar proportion of a species in both a sparse and a dense population. | 16 | Which age structure is most likely to support future population growth, assuming survival and reproduction conditions remain similar?<br />A) A broad base of young individuals<br />B) A population made up only of post-reproductive adults<br />C) Equal numbers at every age with no young<br />D) A population with no reproductive individuals | **A) A broad base of young individuals**<br />Many young individuals can enter reproductive ages later. | 17 | A species produces many small offspring and provides little parental care. This pattern is most consistent with:<br />A) High investment in each offspring<br />B) Low investment in each offspring<br />C) No reproduction<br />D) Obligate mutualism | **B) Low investment in each offspring**<br />Producing many small offspring with little care is a reproductive strategy with relatively low parental investment per offspring. |
| 18 | Which statement about a species' ecological niche is most accurate?<br />A) It is only the place where the species is found<br />B) It includes how the species uses resources and interacts with conditions<br />C) It is identical to the species' population size<br />D) It describes only its predators | **B) It includes how the species uses resources and interacts with conditions**<br />A niche describes a species' ecological role and resource use, including its responses to and effects on environmental conditions. |
| 19 | Two species use the same limited food source in the same habitat. | 20 | In a food chain, arrows conventionally point from:<br />A) Consumer to the organism it eats<br />B) Food source to the organism receiving energy<br />C) Predator to prey only<br />D) Decomposer to sunlight | **B) Food source to the organism receiving energy**<br />The arrow indicates the direction of energy transfer: from the organism or material eaten to the consumer. |

## How to revise this MDCAT-Style Ecology and Evolution Biology Practice set

1. When a question names an ecological level, identify what is included: one organism, one species' population, interacting populations, or the whole biotic and abiotic system.
2. For population calculations, write the formula first and keep units consistent; density is individuals divided by area or volume.
3. Draw arrows from food to consumer when tracing energy. Energy moves through trophic levels, while elements such as carbon are recycled.
4. For interaction questions, record the effect on each species as positive, negative or neutral before naming the relationship.
5. Separate an organism's response during its lifetime from inherited population change across generations; natural selection acts on heritable variation.
6. Review incorrect answers by noting the mistaken distinction, such as drift versus selection or primary versus secondary succession.
