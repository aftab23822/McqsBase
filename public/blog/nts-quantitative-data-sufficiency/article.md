## How to revise this NTS-Style Quantitative Data Sufficiency set

1. For every missed item, note whether you treated a possible value as the only possible value; sufficiency requires a definite result.
2. Cover Statement II while testing Statement I, then reverse the process. Do not let information from one statement leak into the other test.
3. When the question asks for a yes-or-no result, look for a definite yes or a definite no; a statement that leaves both outcomes possible is insufficient.
4. For geometry and rate questions, write the governing relationship first, such as area = length × width or rate = distance ÷ time.
5. Review the standard answer choices before another timed attempt so you can focus on evaluating the information rather than decoding the options.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does data sufficiency mean in these questions?

It means deciding whether the information provided determines the requested value or a definite yes-or-no answer. You are judging sufficiency, not simply solving as quickly as possible.

### Should I combine the two statements immediately?

No. Test Statement I alone and Statement II alone first. Combine them only if neither statement independently gives a definite answer.

### Can a statement be sufficient without giving a numerical value directly?

Yes. A relationship can be sufficient if it uniquely determines the requested value. For example, a known sum and a known difference can determine two unknowns together.

### Are these questions an official NTS paper or syllabus?

No. They are original, independent NTS-style practice. Test formats and arrangements can vary, so consult the latest official notice for current information.
