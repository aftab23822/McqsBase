| 1 | Neither the clerk nor the assistants ___ available to answer the query.<br />A) is<br />B) are<br />C) was<br />D) has been | **B) are**<br />With neither/nor, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject. Assistants is plural, so are is appropriate. |
| 2 | Every one of the application forms ___ a signature.<br />A) require<br />B) requires<br />C) have required<br />D) were requiring | **B) requires**<br />Every one is grammatically singular, even though it refers to several forms. It therefore takes requires. |
| 3 | A number of applicants ___ asked for additional time.<br />A) has<br />B) is<br />C) have<br />D) was | **C) have**<br />A number of means several, so the plural noun applicants controls the verb. The usual construction is a number of applicants have. |
| 4 | The number of vacant positions ___ lower than expected.<br />A) are<br />B) is<br />C) were<br />D) have been | **B) is**<br />The subject is the singular phrase the number, not the plural noun positions. Use is. |
| 5 | The news about the selection ___ encouraging.<br />A) are<br />B) were<br />C) is<br />D) have been | **C) is**<br />News ends in s but is treated as a singular, uncountable noun in standard English. |
| 6 | Economics ___ one of the subjects in her course.<br />A) are<br />B) is<br />C) were<br />D) have | **B) is**<br />The name of an academic subject, including economics, normally takes a singular verb. |
| 7 | Each boy and girl ___ given a separate entry card.<br />A) were<br />B) have been<br />C) was<br />D) are | **C) was**<br />Each makes the subject singular. The sentence describes a completed past action, so was is the matching form. |
| 8 | The committee ___ its final recommendation as a single group.<br />A) have submitted<br />B) has submitted<br />C) are submitting<br />D) were submitting | **B) has submitted**<br />The sentence presents the committee as one unit, so the singular has submitted agrees with it. |
| 9 | The police ___ the damaged equipment at the site.<br />A) is examining<br />B) has examined<br />C) are examining<br />D) was examined | **C) are examining**<br />Police is normally a plural noun in this meaning and takes a plural verb: are examining. |
| 10 | Neither the teacher nor the students ___ ready to leave.<br />A) was<br />B) is<br />C) were<br />D) has been | **C) were**<br />With neither/nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Students is plural, so were is correct. |
| 11 | She ___ in Lahore since 2021.<br />A) lived<br />B) has lived<br />C) is living<br />D) had lived | **B) has lived**<br />Since 2021 marks a period that began in the past and continues to the present, which commonly takes the present perfect. |
| 12 | By the time we reached the station, the train ___.<br />A) leaves<br />B) has left<br />C) had left<br />D) was leaving tomorrow | **C) had left**<br />The train's departure happened before another past event, our arrival. The past perfect marks the earlier event. |
| 13 | If you heat ice, it ___.<br />A) melted<br />B) will melted<br />C) melts<br />D) would melt yesterday | **C) melts**<br />A general fact uses the zero conditional: present simple in both clauses. Thus, if you heat ice, it melts. |
| 14 | If I ___ the principal, I would extend the library hours.<br />A) am<br />B) was<br />C) were<br />D) will be | **C) were**<br />For an unreal or hypothetical condition, standard formal English uses were with I: If I were the principal. |
| 15 | If she had left earlier, she ___ the bus.<br />A) would catch<br />B) would have caught<br />C) will catch<br />D) had caught | **B) would have caught**<br />This is a past unreal condition: if + past perfect is paired with would have + past participle. |
| 16 | When the meeting ___, I will send you the notes.<br />A) will finish<br />B) finishes<br />C) finished<br />D) would finish | **B) finishes**<br />In a future time clause introduced by when, use the present simple; the main clause can use will. |
| 17 | The technician ___ the printer at the moment.<br />A) repairs<br />B) repaired<br />C) is repairing<br />D) has repaired yesterday | **C) is repairing**<br />At the moment signals an action happening now, so the present continuous is the appropriate tense. |
| 18 | We ___ the notice on Monday.<br />A) see<br />B) have seen<br />C) saw<br />D) had see | **C) saw**<br />On Monday refers to a completed past time in this context, so use the simple past saw. |
| 19 | My uncle ___ in Multan, but he moved to Quetta last year.<br />A) used to live<br />B) is used to live<br />C) use to lived<br />D) was used living | **A) used to live**<br />Used to + base verb describes a past situation that no longer applies. Live is the base form. |
| 20 | At 8 a.m., Sana ___ for the bus for thirty minutes.<br />A) waits<br />B) has waited<br />C) had been waiting<br />D) will wait | **C) had been waiting**<br />The waiting continued up to a stated past time, 8 a.m. The past perfect continuous expresses that duration. |

## How to revise this Competitive Exam English Grammar set

1. When a sentence contains either/or, neither/nor or an intervening phrase, locate the subject that controls the verb before choosing an option.
2. Underline time expressions such as since, by the time and yesterday; use them to check tense and event order.
3. Learn prepositions as part of common combinations, such as depend on, capable of and congratulate someone on something.
4. For modifier questions, ask who is doing the action described at the beginning of the sentence; that noun should follow the introductory phrase.
5. In reported speech, check whether the reporting verb is in the past and adjust pronouns and verb tense to match the stated context.
6. For editing questions, read the whole sentence after selecting an option and check that paired structures and list items follow the same grammatical pattern.
