## How to revise this MDCAT Genetics and Inheritance set

1. Write the parental genotypes before making a Punnett square; do not infer a genotype from a dominant phenotype alone.
2. For probability questions, identify whether the question asks for one child or several children, then multiply independent probabilities when appropriate.
3. Keep allele notation consistent, especially for X-linked traits and the ABO blood-group alleles.
4. Practise tracing DNA to mRNA to protein, checking base-pairing rules and the direction of each step.
5. When reviewing a mutation question, distinguish a change in DNA sequence from its possible effect on a protein or phenotype.

No. They practise selected genetics concepts. Check the latest official notice and syllabus for the current test's scope and arrangements.

### What is the best way to approach an unfamiliar inheritance pattern?

Identify the parents' known traits, list plausible genotypes, and test which cross can produce the observed offspring. Avoid assuming that a dominant phenotype always identifies a single genotype.
