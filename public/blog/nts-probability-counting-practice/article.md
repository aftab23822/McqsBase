## How to revise this NTS Probability and Counting Aptitude set

1. For each probability question, write the total number of equally likely outcomes before counting favourable outcomes.
2. Use the complement for phrases such as at least one: subtract the probability of none from 1.
3. Before using a permutation or combination, decide whether changing the order creates a different outcome.
4. For draws without replacement, update both the favourable count and the total count after the first draw.
5. For committee questions, count each allowed group composition with combinations, then divide by the total number of possible committees.
6. Redo missed calculations without looking at the explanation, then identify whether the error came from counting, the formula, or arithmetic.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should I use a permutation instead of a combination?

Use a permutation when order matters, such as assigning distinct roles or arranging people in a line. Use a combination when only the selected group matters, such as choosing committee members.

### How do I handle probability questions that say at least one?

Often it is simpler to calculate the probability of none and subtract it from 1. For example, with repeated independent trials, find the chance of failure on every trial, then take its complement.

### What is the difference between drawing with and without replacement?

With replacement, the item is returned before the next draw, so the probabilities remain unchanged. Without replacement, the total number of items and possibly the number of favourable items change after each draw.
