This independent practice set develops critical reading and argument skills through short claims and scenarios. The questions ask you to identify conclusions, distinguish evidence from assumptions, test cause-and-effect claims, and judge which information strengthens or weakens an argument. Read each prompt closely: an option can sound reasonable yet still go beyond what the evidence supports. | 1 | A town installed brighter streetlights on several roads, and reported night-time thefts later fell on those roads. ## How to revise this HEC LAT Critical Reading and Argument Reasoning set

1. For each missed question, write the argument's evidence and conclusion separately; many errors come from treating the conclusion as a fact already proved.
2. When asked for an assumption, test whether the argument would lose its support if that unstated idea were false.
3. For causal claims, look for a comparison group and ask whether another change could explain the result.
4. Reject answers that introduce details such as motives, time periods, or universal claims that the prompt never establishes.
5. When choosing evidence, prefer information that directly tests the stated claim over information that is merely related to the topic.
